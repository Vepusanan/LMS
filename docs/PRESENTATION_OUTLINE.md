# Presentation outline — 10 slides

1. **The challenge: a student portal that gets in the way**  
   Show the original home/dashboard screenshots. Describe the real student goals: courses, deadlines, classes, registration and messages. State frontend-only scope.
2. **What we observed**  
   Highlight eight concrete problems: buried deadline, conflicting timeline, mixed semesters, truncated titles, generic topics, unexplained grades, privacy noise, weak mobile/keyboard support. Separate observation from assumed impact.
3. **Designing around people**  
   First-year, low-vision/keyboard and phone scenarios. Explain attention, memory, recognition and motor demands.
4. **A calmer visual system**  
   Reference image beside the new dashboard. Explain charcoal navigation, lavender accents, meaningful course illustrations, status labels and consistent spacing.
5. **From “Where is it?” to “Here’s what’s next”**  
   Demonstrate next deadline, unread messages, timetable and shared source dates. Include the original January deadline conflict and the explicit closed state.
6. **Make mistakes recoverable**  
   Demonstrate invalid file validation, local submission feedback, completion undo and registration review. Explain why local actions are labelled simulations.
7. **Emerging technology with a purpose**  
   Demo assistant prompts and linked answers; show unsupported-voice fallback/text alternatives. Cover trust, privacy and human handoff.
8. **Responsive and inclusive by design**  
   Desktop and 360px screenshots. Demonstrate mobile navigation, dark/high-contrast settings, text scaling, keyboard focus and grade cards. Distinguish automated coverage from full accessibility certification.
9. **How we will evaluate it**  
   Show the five tasks and the usability timer/CSV export. Leave participant observations blank until testing. Report automated checks separately from human results.
10. **What we learned and what comes next**  
    Use personalised reflection after real testing. Answer the closing question: modern AI is not good HCI if it undermines clarity or autonomy. Prioritise revisions using observed severity and frequency.
